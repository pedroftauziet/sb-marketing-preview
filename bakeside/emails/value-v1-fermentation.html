The hardest part of sourdough isn’t the recipe — it’s knowing the moment your starter is actually at peak. Too early and it’s underpowered; too late and it’s already past it. Either way, the bake pays for it.

And the float test is unreliable, while the clock lies: “8 hours” or “double in size” are averages — your kitchen temperature, your flour and your starter’s strength move the real window by hours. So how do you actually read it?

3 signs your starter is actually at peak

1.  Domed and pulling at the sides — not flat.

2.  A web of bubbles all through the jar, not just on top.

3.  It’s hit its high mark and is just starting to relax. That’s peak — more reliable than the float test.
