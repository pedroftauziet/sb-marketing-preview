Your kitchen is rewriting every recipe

Two bakers, same recipe, same flour — one pulls out an airy loaf, the other a dense one. The difference usually isn’t skill. It’s temperature. Fermentation is alive, and warmth is its accelerator: a few degrees warmer and the dough races; a few cooler and it crawls. So a recipe’s “proof 4 hours” really means “proof 4 hours at ~24°C” — and your kitchen is almost never exactly there.

We see this constantly. Across the thousands of bakes people run through BakeSide, the same recipe lands hours apart depending on the kitchen — and the bakers who adjust for it are the ones getting the good crumb.

What that means for your next loaf

Warm kitchen (26°C+) — it’ll move faster than the recipe says. Check early; don’t trust the clock.

Cool kitchen (under 20°C) — it’ll take longer. Give it time, or find a warmer corner.

Read the dough, not the timer — domed, jiggly, airy is ready, whatever the clock says.
