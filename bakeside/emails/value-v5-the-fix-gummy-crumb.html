Why your crumb turns out gummy

You cut into a loaf that looked perfect — and the middle is dense, damp, almost gluey. It’s the most disheartening ending in baking, and most people blame their oven or their recipe. But a gummy crumb almost never means you did the bake wrong. It means the loaf wasn’t ready — to be baked, or to be cut. Three things cause it, and all three are fixable.

3 reasons a crumb turns gummy
You cut it warm
Bread keeps cooking as it cools. Slice into a warm loaf and the still-setting starches smear into that gummy paste. Wait until it’s fully cool — hours, not minutes.
It was underproofed
A tight, damp, heavy crumb usually means fermentation never finished. The dough went in before it was airy and alive. Give it more time, or a warmer corner.
It was underbaked
Pull it too early and the center never sets. Bake to a deep, confident colour — the loaf should feel light and sound hollow when you tap the base.
